# Candidates — "How Many Tabs Do You Have Open?"

The entry is a single number in digits and nothing else (1–5 characters, gate-checked).
The judge (a self-described browser tab hoarder) compares two counts at a time on
these criteria, most important first:

1. Reads as a real count of open browser tabs.
2. Exactly one number in digits — no words, no punctuation, no second number.
3. Surprising: far higher, far lower, or stranger than what the field would guess.
4. Believable as an honest answer — not an absurd figure picked to shock.
5. Hints at a life behind it — a habit or a situation — with no explanation.

"Size for its own sake does not count." Byte-identical entries share one rate, and
the earlier receipt places above its copies, so heavily duplicated meme numbers
(42, 69, 100, 420, 666, 777, 1000…) carry a duplication penalty in the notes even
when the judge might like them.

Scoring below: C1 real-count feel /5, C3 surprise /5, C4 believability /5,
C5 life-hint /5 — total /20. C2 is pass/fail; every candidate here passes it.

## Tournament (top 8, judged pairwise on the brief's ordered criteria)

Seeds: 347 (20), 317 (19), 412 (19), 1 (18), 187 (18), 213 (18), 289 (18), 389 (18).

- QF1: **347 vs 389** — both real, exact-format. First difference is C3/C5:
  389 is as surprising, but 347 sits in the sweet spot where the hoard is deep
  yet every digit feels counted rather than reached for. 347 wins.
- QF2: **317 vs 412** — 412 is bigger, but size for its own sake does not count;
  317's prime, un-round specificity reads more counted. 317 wins.
- QF3: **1 vs 213** — 1 is startling-low and disciplined, but it is the single
  most duplicated text in any such field and hints at less life than 213
  accumulated intentions. 213 wins.
- QF4: **187 vs 289** — near-identical profiles; 289 is the stranger, deeper
  count while staying fully believable. 289 wins.
- SF1: **347 vs 317** — same family. 347 is deeper into hoarder territory
  (C3) while keeping C4 intact; the extra thirty tabs hint at one more
  abandoned project. 347 wins.
- SF2: **213 vs 289** — 289 is higher and stranger without losing honesty. 289 wins.
- Final: **347 vs 289** — both prime, both specific, both believable. 347 is
  the more surprising count (C3) and the stronger confession (C5): 289 is a
  busy person; 347 is a person whose browser is an archive of intentions.

## Winner

**347** — 3 characters, digits only. A prime, oddly exact count deep in hoarder
territory yet well inside believability: it reads as counted, not chosen, and it
hints at a whole life of "I'll get back to that one" without a word of explanation.
